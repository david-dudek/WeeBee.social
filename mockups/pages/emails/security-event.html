Security notices

Two worked examples in one file, the same pattern reset-code.html already uses for more than one state: SPEC §4.6.1 names three event types that "generate account/security notifications" and "carry absolute timestamps wherever they appear" — new-device login, password change, and email change. The third, email change, has its own dedicated pair of templates (email-change-code.html and email-change-notice.html) per this session's own item 4, so the two shown here are the other two.

From:
WeeBee <notifications@weebee.social>
To:
David Dudek <david@example.com>
Subject:
New sign-in to your WeeBee account

New sign-in to your account

Your WeeBee account was just signed in to from a new device, at 2026-08-04 21:14 UTC.

If this was you, there's nothing else to do.

If it wasn't, open WeeBee yourself and start a password reset right away.

WeeBee will never email you a link to log in or reset your password — only a code you type in yourself.

From:
WeeBee <notifications@weebee.social>
To:
David Dudek <david@example.com>
Subject:
Your WeeBee password was changed

Your password was changed

The password on your WeeBee account was just changed, at 2026-08-04 21:14 UTC.

If this was you, there's nothing else to do.

If it wasn't, open WeeBee yourself and start a password reset right away — you don't need your old password to do that.

WeeBee will never email you a link to log in or reset your password — only a code you type in yourself.

— WeeBee

SPEC §4.6.1: "these events carry absolute timestamps wherever they appear... 'was that login me?' is not a question anyone can answer with 'several hours ago.'" The example timestamp, 2026-08-04 21:14 UTC, is the exact form §12.3 itself gives. The checkable promise (§4.6.1, verbatim, per mockups/CRIB.md §2) appears on both, since either scenario is exactly the moment a worried recipient is most exposed to a look-alike follow-up email.
