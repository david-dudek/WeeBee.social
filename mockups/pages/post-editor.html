Edit post

You can change a post's words, its picture, whether it's shown in the exact-spacing mode, and its hashtags. A comment can only have its words changed, and only by whoever wrote it.

SPEC §7.8 draws that line precisely: a post's text, image, preformatted toggle and hashtags are editable; nothing else on a post is. A comment's text is editable by its own author only. The five sections below each show one editing state from David's own point of view, since editing is always something an author does to their own words.

1. Editing a feed post's words

Here's David fixing a typo in a feed post he already sent.

A few days ago edited a few minutes ago

Once you save, this post will always show "edited" next to its age, along with roughly when that last happened. There's nothing to compare it against and no history to scroll back through — just the fact that it changed, and about when.

SPEC §7.8: the "edited" marker is permanent, real text, with no version history and no diff — "this is not an archive." Saving here re-runs every content check a new post would (length caps, the URL allowlist, whitespace rules) but does not re-notify anyone, reset the 90-day expiry clock, or move the post in the feed's order (§7.8's five invariants). This post carries no hashtags of its own; see sections 3 and 4 below for what editing hashtags does on a post where they matter — a profile post. On a feed post like this one, any hashtags are decorative ranking signals only (SPEC §11.2) and editing them here would not move the audience at all.

2. Replacing a post's image

David is swapping the picture on an older post for a better one. Replacing a picture always asks for a fresh description of it — we never carry the old description over or guess a new one.

Alternative text for the new image

SPEC §7.8, §16.3, §9.4: replacing a post's image re-prompts for alternative text every time, exactly as the composer and the gallery do — a deliberate choice, never carried over from the image being replaced and never auto-filled.

SPEC §7.8, §7.9, §11.3, v1.16: ticking #cornwall on top of the two tags this post already carried is the widening action — the same live audience line the composer uses, updating as tags change, present unconditionally rather than only on "dangerous" choices. Because the post already has comments (reused verbatim from post-profile-tagged.html, the same post as seen by Priya), widening the tags re-exposes those comments to whichever new friends-of-friends #cornwall reaches, and every existing commenter — Alice, Tom and Priya — is notified: "David added a hashtag to a post you commented on. More people can see it now." That notification is not shown as its own state here; it belongs on the recipients' own feeds, which this session does not rebuild.

SPEC §7.8, v1.16: removing a tag narrows the audience, and narrowing needs no consent from anyone — no notice appears here, and nobody who loses access is told. Contrast with section 3 above: the same live line renders either way, but only widening triggers the comments notice and the commenter notification.

5. Editing and deleting comments

You can always edit your own comments. On a post you wrote yourself, you can also delete anyone's comment — but you can't rewrite someone else's words. Deleting is yours to do here; rewriting isn't.

SPEC §7.8, §8.1: "deleting someone's words is a host's prerogative, rewriting them is ventriloquism." These three comments are the same ones already shown on the dinner post's single-post view.
