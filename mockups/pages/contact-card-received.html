SPEC §10.4, rewritten in v1.30 and clarified in v1.31. This page is one card page per (owner, viewer) pair, at its own permission-checked address (§9.3), reached from the owner's About tab (§9.1), resolved live on every visit and never stored. It is not a reply, and nothing about it is delivered: the auto-reply of §10.4 opens this page rather than handing over a copy of it. M5 built this file against the pre-1.30 §10.4, which described a one-time reply; R3 rebuilt it rather than editing it, because the framing was the thing that was wrong.

Coming back to it

You can open this page as often as you like. David isn't told when you do, and it doesn't count as asking him again.

If he changes what he shares with you, this page changes with it. Something that was here last month may not be here now, and nothing will point that out — the page simply shows what he is sharing today.

§10.4, both halves. Returning is not a new request: no notification — §12.1's contact-card-request notification fires on the request, not on a visit — no rate-limit consumption (§13.6), and the owner is told nothing. §10.4 says why the obvious implementation matters: treating every view as a request would make a friend's second look at a phone number an event in the owner's feed. The honest consequence is rendered above as real user-facing copy rather than only as commentary, because §10.4 states it plainly and it is the cost of live resolution: a friend who returns can tell that something changed, because it did, and nothing announces it.

Why the resolution is live, and never stored — §10.4 gives three separate grounds. A stored answer would defeat §10.3, whose whole discipline is that over-sharing is unrecoverable, so an item switched off has to disappear from the person it was switched off for. A stored answer would be a message, and the only one on the platform — §10.1 refuses the category. And a card does not expire (§9.7), so a stored copy would be a copy with no clock at all.

What Henry sees

You haven't shared anything on your contact card with Henry.

The empty case, and it is not an error — a real, honest empty state. David has individually turned off all four items that default on for friends generally — the phone, the unlabelled second number, Signal and "My photos" — and the remaining two are off by default and never switched on for him. An empty card carries no report action at all (§13.2): there is nothing on it to report, and a complaint about the person rather than the card is a profile report. The specific overrides producing this are a constructed worked example, the same kind of deliberate fact this track has invented before to demonstrate a mechanism — they say nothing about David and Henry's actual relationship.

The report action, and its three placement rules (§13.2, v1.30). One real <button> per item, never one for the card, with the visible text "Report this item" given verbatim by SPEC. It is available to anyone this page is available to — a current friend whose request has been answered — and never to the card's owner, exactly as the profile report is not. §13.2 argues the per-item placement in terms: a card may carry CONTACT_ITEMS_MAX = 12 items, and an operator opening "someone reported David's card" would have to guess which of the twelve. "The card as a whole" is a reason category on the report form, reachable from any item's button — not a separate control on this page.
