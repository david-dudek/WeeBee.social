Your account will be deleted

SPEC §4.8: the last two of the four inactivity warnings, at 670 and 700 days since last login — "60 and 30 days before deletion" — "state the absolute deletion date... never 'in a couple of months'... a deletion warning that cannot say when is not a warning." Both examples below point at the same eventual deletion date, since they are two successive warnings about one account's one deletion, 30 days apart; only the wording of what's still ahead of it differs.

First deletion warning — sent 670 days since last login, 60 days before deletion.

From:
WeeBee <notifications@weebee.social>
To:
David Dudek <david@example.com>
Subject:
Your WeeBee account will be deleted on 12 March 2027

You haven't logged in to WeeBee in a long time. If that doesn't change, your account — everything in it — will be deleted on 12 March 2027.

Logging in is all it takes to cancel this. Once you're in, there's nothing else you need to do.

If you'd rather your account be deleted, you don't need to do anything at all — it will happen on its own, on that date.

Second and final deletion warning — sent 700 days since last login, 30 days before the same deletion date.

From:
WeeBee <notifications@weebee.social>
To:
David Dudek <david@example.com>
Subject:
Your WeeBee account will be deleted on 12 March 2027

This is the last notice before it happens: your WeeBee account, and everything in it, will be deleted on 12 March 2027, unless you log in before then.

Logging in is all it takes to cancel this. Once you're in, there's nothing else you need to do.

If you'd rather your account be deleted, you don't need to do anything at all — it will happen on its own, on that date.

— WeeBee

SPEC §4.8's own worked example gives the date without a year — "your account will be deleted on 12 March" — this mockup adds a plausible year, 2027, for concreteness, since a real email needs one. INACTIVITY_DELETE_DAYS = 730, INACTIVITY_WARN_DAYS = 180 / 365 / 670 / 700. No link and no code appear on either message: neither is asking the recipient to complete anything with a code, so per this session's rule 1 the honest option is no link at all — logging in is an ordinary, self-initiated visit to the site, the same posture SPEC's own checkable promise encourages everywhere else.
