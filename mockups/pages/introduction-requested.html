Alice asks you to introduce her to Henry

SPEC §5.5(b): Alice, David's friend, wants an introduction to Henry, David's friend — someone Alice doesn't yet know but can already see. She named him with a picker, not a text field: no message, no note, nothing written to David or to Henry.

Alice could only point at someone she can already see: a friend-of-friend who shares a mutual friend and, in practice, a hashtag with her (§11.3), or someone who happens to be a mutual friend between her and David. She can't name a stranger three hops away — that ceiling is permanent (§11.1) — and the picker itself never offers anyone outside it.

Respond

Agreeing sends Henry the standard introduction. Declining sends nothing, to either of them.

§5.5(b): "M's decline is silent." If you decline, Alice is never told you were asked, or that you said no — the same rule that governs every decline on this platform.

If you agree

Flow (a) then runs with Alice's side already accepted; only Henry decides. Henry receives the same introduction card the broker flow already renders for Ben's view of Grace — "David wants to introduce you to Alice," Alice's basic-tier profile, and the auto-generated context — with Accept and Decline. It is not rebuilt a second time here, since it is the identical component fed from Alice's data instead of Grace's.

Nothing in §5.5 says whether you, having agreed, are later told how it went. This page states only what SPEC gives — that your decline is silent, and that agreeing hands the decision entirely to Henry — and does not invent an outcome notification beyond that.
