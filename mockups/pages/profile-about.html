Spent most of this year rediscovering things I used to be impatient about: bread that needs a day to prove, a garden that ignores my schedule entirely, a trail up the ridge that hasn't gotten any shorter since I was twenty. None of it is efficient and I've stopped apologizing for that.

We keep saying we'll finally walk the whole coast path together instead of just the one weekend a year we manage — https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=12/50.24/-5.05 is the stretch I'm angling for next spring, no excuses this time. Always happy to talk baking disasters, terrible garden puns, or where to find decent coffee near the ridge trailhead.

Before Alice has asked

Nothing to write — asking is the whole of it.

The request is a picker with no text field (§10.4). Answering is automatic, and what it opens is a page Alice can return to for as long as the friendship lasts, not a reply she is handed once. This second state is rendered here only to show the control's other face; a real About tab carries one or the other, never both at once.

Why this was safe to add, and it is worth not making a later reader re-derive it. §9.1 states that "the contact-card control is a control, not a field, and the basic-tier invariant below is unaffected." That invariant is load-bearing: the header plus About minus the extended bio must stay exactly §9.2's basic tier and exactly what §5.2's friend-request card shows, all three rendered from one component with one friends-only flag — if they drift apart, the screening arguments of §5.2 and §13.1 quietly stop being true. A control adds no field to the About tab and appears on none of the three surfaces the invariant governs, so the invariant is untouched. It sits here rather than in the header because the card is per-friend (§10.3) and the request has to live somewhere a friend will find it — and because §9.1's header is closed: the photo, the name and the report action, and nothing else.
