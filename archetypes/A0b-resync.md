## Step 2 — correct fact 12, in both files that carry it

Fact 12 appears **twice**, worded identically: `PLAN.md` §4 and `A0-brief.md` Step 3's quoted
block. Both must change, and they must end up character-for-character identical to each other.

It currently reads:

> 12. **There are no private messages.** Instead you keep a contact card — up to 12 phone
>     numbers, email addresses and messenger links — and decide, item by item and friend by
>     friend, who sees what. A friend requests it with a button and the system replies with
>     exactly their version. The conversation itself happens somewhere else. (§10)

**Both halves are now false.** The item kinds changed in v1.29 (§10.2: *"phone numbers, email
addresses, and links"* — any URL, plus a short label on every item). And the answer stopped
being a reply in v1.30 (§10.4): it is a **page**, one per (owner, viewer) pair, resolved live on
every visit and never stored, which the friend returns to for as long as the friendship lasts.

Draft replacement — verify every clause against §10.2 and §10.4, then use it in both files:

> 12. **There are no private messages.** Instead you keep a contact card — up to 12 items, each
>     one a phone number, an email address or a link, each with a short label you write — and
>     decide, item by item and friend by friend, who sees what. A friend asks for it with a
>     button, with nothing to type, and that opens a page they can come back to for as long as
>     you are friends. It is worked out fresh every time they open it: switch an item off and it
>     is simply gone from their page, and nothing announces the change. The conversation itself
>     happens somewhere else. (§10)

Keep the plain, non-technical register the other thirteen facts use. A character is being told
what the product does, not read a specification.

## Step 4 — the delegation rule, in `A7-susan.md`

`A7-susan.md` is the pilot, and `PLAN.md` §13 says the nine remaining prompts are structural
copies of it. So a rule added here copies into all ten — which is the point, because the blind
spot was never Susan's alone.

Add a new subsection **immediately after "The honesty rule"** and before "Step 5 — the probes",
headed `## The delegation rule`. Draft:

> **The delegation rule.** When this character runs into something WeeBee will not hold — sixty
> photographs against a gallery of eight, a video, a file, an album — the interviewer must **name
> the way out**: keep it wherever they already keep it, and post about it here with the address.
> Then ask the second question, and record the answer honestly: **would they actually do that?**
>
> **Two things this rule is not.** It is **not a rescue.** Offering the handoff is not the same as
> the character taking it, and this character is entitled to answer *"then what am I here for"* —
> **if they do, that is the finding**, and it is worth more to the founder than agreement. The
> rule exists so that nobody is asked to judge half a product; it does not exist to talk anyone
> round.
>
> And it is **not a hyperlink.** Since v1.29 a photo-host address in a post arrives as an address
> the reader **copies**, not a link they tap. So what is actually on offer is *"post about the
> album and paste the address, and your family copies it"* — one deliberate extra step for every
> person who wants to look. Whether that is good enough for this character is exactly what the
> interview should find out rather than assume.

Then **extend probe 4**, which is where the wall is certain to be hit, so the third beat is
explicit rather than left to the rule alone. It currently reads:

> 4. A family reunion produced sixty photographs. Ask what she does with them here. Then tell her
>    the gallery holds eight and a post carries one image, and ask again.

Add one sentence, in the same register — something close to: *"Then offer her the way out — the
album stays wherever she keeps it, and she posts about it here with the address her family copies
— and ask whether she would actually do that."* Do not add a fourth beat guessing her answer.

**Check what you wrote against `PLAN.md` §7's mechanical test:** read it and ask whether it would
be a fair thing to say to someone who turned out to *love* the mechanic. A rule that offers the
handoff passes. A rule that implies the handoff settles the objection does not.
