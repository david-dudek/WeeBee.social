Log in

Enter the email address and password you used when you joined WeeBee.

WeeBee will never email you a link to log in or reset your password — only a code you type in yourself.

SPEC §4.6.1's checkable anti-phishing promise, quoted verbatim, shown "at the relevant touchpoints (login, reset, help)" — this is one of them. It's rendered here as prominent real text rather than fine print, because its value depends on being seen: it's what makes a look-alike "click here to log in" email self-evidently fake, no matter how convincing the sending domain looks.

Forgot your password?

SPEC §4.5, §4.6: login is by email address, never by display name — names carry no uniqueness requirement (§4.5) and couldn't work as a login identifier. The reset link has to live here, on the login screen itself, rather than inside an email, because §4.6.1 requires the user to reach the reset page "by navigating to the site themselves."

No CAPTCHA or other human-challenge control appears anywhere on this page, or anywhere else in this mockup set. SPEC §4.6.1 rejects the mainstream options as ineffective against phishing and incompatible with the tracking ban; §16.4 repeats the ban; §17 lists "no CAPTCHA or third-party human-challenge" among the platform's explicit non-goals.
