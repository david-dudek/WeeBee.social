9. An unapproved link

Nothing above this line is missing. SPEC §7.2.4 gives a link three possible outcomes, and only one of them is an error: an allowlisted address in scope for this surface becomes a clickable link, a blocklisted address is refused (section 10 below), and an address on neither list — this one — is simply delivered differently, as an inert copy box in the published post. §7.2.4 is emphatic that this is "a different rendering, not a refusal", with "no warning, no apology, no error styling, no 'this link is not approved' note beside it." So this section carries no aria-invalid, no .field-error and no hint: the composer says nothing, because there is nothing to say. §7.2.4 explains why the silence is load-bearing — on a platform that already says no to a great deal, a system that also scolds each time is experienced as scolding generally, and the person who feels scolded is the ordinary member posting a link to their own photo album, which is exactly the post above. bit.ly is the honest example here rather than in section 10: §7.2.3 says a URL shortener is never allowlistable, because it makes the destination unknowable — which makes it permanently a copy box, not a refusal, unless the operator has separately blocklisted it. For the rendering this produces once published, see a feed post and mockups/partials/_post.html. Before v1.29 this section refused this address outright, and its commentary stated the rule that the allowlist governs delivery; the allowlist now governs clickability, and delivery is governed by the blocklist alone.

10. A blocklisted link

The one link outcome that really is an error, and the only one that keeps an error affordance. §7.2.4: the author is warned before the post is published, not after — the check runs at composition and again at save, on the create and edit paths alike (§7.8's invariant 4). The message is the wording registered in mockups/CRIB.md §2, which is invented: §7.2.4 says what it must do — say plainly that the address cannot be posted here and that the fix is to remove it, and "name no appeal, because there is none" — and gives no words for it (see mockups/NOTES.md). It offers no reason for the block and no route to contest it, both deliberately.

This address can't be posted on WeeBee. Remove the link to save your post.

blocklisted.example.com is a stand-in on the reserved documentation domain example.com, not a real site being accused of anything. §7.2.3 states that what belongs on the blocklist "is not specified here" — it is operational judgment the operator exercises case by case, in the same way NAME_BLOCKLIST's contents are — so naming any real domain here would invent a policy neither document states. The list itself is an operator-maintained table, never a constant in code, which is why it is written in plain words above rather than as a code-style constant name.

Switching to a blog post clears the audience you picked — a blog post has no recipient list, only its hashtags.

Switching to a feed post: hashtags stop gating who can see this post. They become decorative until you switch back.

About preformatted posts

A preformatted post renders in a monospace font and keeps runs of spaces exactly as typed — useful for text tables, diagrams, and ASCII art. The trade-offs: exact spacing means we won't tidy up your whitespace for you; long lines never wrap, so a wide table stays wide; and on a narrow screen the post scrolls sideways instead of reflowing. See what a preformatted post looks like once it's published.

Linked directly from the preformatted toggle above. The horizontal scroll on narrow screens is the platform's one documented exception to the reflow requirement (SPEC §7.2.1, §16.3).
