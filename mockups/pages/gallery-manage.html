Manage your photos

This is your own view of your Photos tab — the one place you can add, replace, reorder and remove pictures, and change your profile photo. Friends see the result on the Photos tab; only you see the controls below.

SPEC §9.4, the Photos tab in its owner-facing state. The eight gallery images and their alternative text below are the same ones already shown on profile-photos.html (Alice's friend view) — this page adds the management controls that view deliberately left out.

Your profile photo

Every WeeBee account always has a profile photo — there's no "no photo" state to leave it in. Yours right now is the one you chose after joining.

Upload a new photo

Uploading tells your friends, once, that you changed it, and it briefly holds new friend requests you send afterward — enough time that your photo can't be used as a rotating billboard aimed at people who haven't friended you yet.

Alternative text for this photo

Or choose one of our default pictures

Switching to one of these is free and immediate — nobody is told, and it doesn't affect when you can next send a friend request.

Default pictures

SPEC §9.4, §12.1, §13.6: DEFAULT_AVATAR_SET images are original artwork, never photographs of a person, and their alternative text is written by the operator rather than the uploader — the one deliberate exception to this section's uploader-authored rule. Every new account starts on the designated default member (Leaf here); selecting any picker image, including switching back to Leaf, triggers no hold and no notification, unlike an upload, which does both.

Your gallery

Up to 8 photos, kept separate from your profile photo and visible to friends only. There's no separate caption field — the description you write for each photo is the caption, and it's what shows underneath the picture when someone opens it.

Your gallery has 8 of 8 photos — the most WeeBee allows. Remove or replace one before you can add another.

GALLERY_MAX = 8 (§9.4, §14). This gallery is already at the cap, which lets this page demonstrate the honest refusal text §16.3 requires rather than inventing a hypothetical over-limit state.

You can reorder photos with the buttons below, or by dragging them — but the buttons always work too, even with a keyboard alone and nothing else.

SPEC §9.4: "a reorder only a mouse can perform is a 2.1.1 failure." Since these mockups carry no scripts, no actual drag-and-drop is implemented here — this states in real text that it would be offered in addition, never instead.
