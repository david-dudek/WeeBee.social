Your contact card

This card is how friends reach you elsewhere — a number, an address, a link — not a rebuilt inbox. Your login email is never included unless you deliberately add it as its own item below; it's a credential, not a card entry.

SPEC §10.1: WeeBee has no free-text direct messaging, which is why this handoff card exists. The login email is governed separately, at §4.6.

The card starts empty

Until you add something, there's nothing here to share. A friend who asks for an empty card opens a page that says so plainly — not an error.

See the empty case, on the card page itself.

Add an item

Up to 12 items total, drawn from phone numbers, email addresses, and links. A link can be any address you like — your photos, a file you're sharing, your own site, a messenger. Whether it ends up as something friends can tap or as an address they copy depends on the address itself, not on you.

Two corrections from v1.29, both substantive. §10.2 previously drew items from "phone numbers, email addresses, and messenger links" and now says "phone numbers, email addresses, and links." And a link item was previously "only accepted from the official domain of a recognized messenger … never a bare URL" — now false: a link item may carry any URL, under the uniform rule of §7.2.4. Clickable if the domain is allowlisted with card scope, an inert copy box otherwise, refused only if blocklisted. The copy for this is written to §7.2.4's no-scolding rule: an address that renders as a copy box is a different rendering, not a refusal, so nothing here flags it as a problem.

The label is new in v1.29 (§10.2): every item carries one, "so that an item is never a naked address." §10.2's own reasoning is worth rendering rather than paraphrasing — the label is new free text on a surface that had none, and the document is rightly hostile to that, so it gets the short bio's treatment: a hard length cap of CARD_ITEM_LABEL_MAX = 40 characters, and NAME_BLOCKLIST screening at every save (§4.5, §9.4). When the label is refused by screening

That label isn't allowed on WeeBee. Please choose a different one.

§10.2 sends the label through NAME_BLOCKLIST at every save and gives no wording for the refusal. This is not a new invented string: it is M7's already-registered wording for a blocked display name — "That name isn't allowed on WeeBee. Please choose a different one" (invite-redeem.html, CRIB.md §2) — with the one noun changed, because it is the same screening list refusing the same shape of thing. Recorded as a reuse in CRIB.md §2 and mockups/NOTES.md rather than registered as a second invention.

Your items

Each item carries its own visibility, set through a three-level cascade: a default for all friends, then any group override, then an individual override, which always wins in either direction. Where a friend belongs to two groups with conflicting settings for the same item, the more restrictive one wins — deny beats allow. Accidental under-sharing is recoverable (see request flags); accidental over-sharing is not.

Added in R3, and it is the whole reason §10.2 was amended in v1.29: before it, WeeBee had nowhere durable to put "here is my photo album" — not the card, not the short bio, not a post. A photo-host link is an ordinary card item and renders on the card page as a copy box, not a hyperlink, because photo hosts are allowlisted for posts and comments rather than for cards (§7.2.3's surface scope). A card with no such item would not show what changed.

Order is load-bearing here: §13.2 freezes the delivered order in a card report, and the two new items are appended rather than inserted so that Alice's resolved card — Signal removed by her individual override — reads phone, email, WhatsApp, the unlabelled number, then "My photos". Real checkboxes throughout, each with a visible label, so the whole cascade is keyboard-operable end to end (§16.3 2.1.1; ARCHITECTURE §3.8) — there is no tri-state control anywhere: an override either exists as its own listed row with its own on/off checkbox, or it does not exist at all, which is what "use the default" means.
