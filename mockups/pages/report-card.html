Report this item

If something on someone's contact card is a problem — what it's labelled, the address or number itself, or how the person has behaved — you can report it. The card stays exactly as it is while the operator looks into it; reporting doesn't hide or change anything by itself.

What's wrong?

What does this report concern?

Categories verbatim from SPEC §13.2: the label · the address or number · the card as a whole · this person's behaviour. "The card as a whole" is reachable from any item's button — it is the category for the case where no single item is the complaint, which is why it needs no control of its own on the card page. "This person's behaviour" is the same last entry the profile list carries, for a card that is a symptom rather than the thing wrong. The free-text note is the same optional note every report already carries, and is not an exception to the platform's no-free-text rule between users — "a report reaches the operator alone."

What this report captures

§13.2 states the frozen/live split as a field table rather than describing it, because it is a security boundary rather than a rendering detail. Three of its rows are decisions rather than bookkeeping, and they are the three worth naming here.

The whole delivered card is frozen, not only the reported item. Every delivered item's kind, value, label and order is captured, along with which position the report points at — a position in the frozen list, never a live pointer to a row the owner can still edit or delete. §13.2's reason is specific: twelve labels of CARD_ITEM_LABEL_MAX = 40 characters are up to 480 characters of author-written free text on a surface that had none before v1.29, and what one label cannot carry, twelve can. An operator shown a single label in isolation would be judging the wrong question.

The cascade is not evidence, and is not captured at all. What the operator needs is what was delivered; how §10.3's cascade arrived at it is the owner's private configuration, and groups are private to their owner by rule — members never know they are in one (§6). Capturing the resolution would disclose David's group structure to answer a question nobody asked.

The freeze is taken at report time, not send time — and this is where a card report differs from §5.2's friend-request card, which freezes at send. §13.2 tested the two rather than inheriting one: a card is auto-answered on request (§10.4), so a send-time freeze would mean storing a copy of every card every friend ever asked for, kept for as long as the friendship lasts, against a report that will almost never come — re-introducing, in a place no user can see, exactly the stored answer §10.4 refuses. The two freezes also defend different things: §5.2's exists because the friend-request card is pushed at up to 20 people a day who never asked for it, while a contact card is pulled, by one friend, who asked. What has to survive here is the evidence, and what endangers it is the owner editing the card the moment a report is filed.

Two rows stay live and are worth naming for the same reason: either party's display name, through the shared helper, because names are never stored on content (§4.5.1); and whether a link item rendered as a hyperlink or as a copy box, read from the allowlist at view time (§7.2.4) — that is the platform's rendering decision, not the author's content, and freezing it would freeze a judgment the operator is meant to be able to change by editing one row.

The freeze lifecycle is §13.3's, unchanged: dismissed and it is purged at once, upheld and it is purged 30 days after, 90 days the hard cap however unreviewed. It must not end early when the owner deletes the item or the friendship lapses — deleting the reported item is the first thing a reported person does — and it needs no extension either, because a card item never expires (§9.7). A frozen card is text and nothing else, so it carries none of the storage cost §5.2's frozen photograph does.

The button above reuses "Send report", the label already established on report-post.html and report-profile.html — SPEC gives no verbatim text for it, and it is reused here rather than restated differently. No new form and no new queue: §13.2 makes a card item a new target type in the one operator queue (§13.5), on the one report form, with no notification to anybody. Reports are deliberately not rate-limited (§13.6): §13.3 has already removed the prize, since reported content stays live while a report is open.
