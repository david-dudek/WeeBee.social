  3. A couple of days ago

    Six months into this sourdough project and I think I've finally stopped lying to myself about what "ready" looks like. The starter is called Gerald, which was a joke that Tom made once and has now been load-bearing for so long that I genuinely can't remember what I called it before that. Gerald has outlived two apartments' worth of windowsills, survived a two-week vacation on nothing but a stranger's fridge and a promise, and has opinions about ambient temperature that I have learned to respect rather than argue with.

    The first twenty loaves were, charitably, doorstops. Dense enough to use as a paperweight, sour in the wrong way, crust like a roof tile. I kept every failure photo because Mom insisted the collection would be funny later, and she was right, though "later" turned out to mean about four months in, right around the time the crumb finally opened up and I stopped needing the kitchen scale to double-check every single gram like it owed me money.

    What changed, as far as I can tell: patience with the bulk ferment, actually reading the dough instead of the clock, and giving up on the idea that a recipe was going to save me from paying attention. Nobody warns you that the hardest ingredient in bread is time you're not allowed to rush, and that the second hardest is trusting a lump of flour and water enough to leave it alone.

    Yesterday's loaf came out of the oven with an ear on the score I actually meant to make, a crumb with real irregular holes instead of the gummy uniform kind, and a crust that crackled audibly cooling on the rack — the sound I'd read about in forums for months and privately assumed was people exaggerating for internet points. It was not an exaggeration. Tom got the first slice, still too hot to cut cleanly, and declared it "finally not a punishment," which from him is basically a five-star review.

    Next project is trying a higher hydration dough without the whole thing turning into soup on the counter, and after that I want to work

  4. About a year ago

    We're moving to Lisbon next month. After two years of talking about it over kitchen-table wine and a spreadsheet neither of us ever quite finished, we finally pulled the trigger: the flat is signed, the moving company is booked, and somehow the cat has strong opinions about the crate we bought her. I keep waiting to feel more nervous about it and mostly just feel relieved — relieved that the deciding is over, that we stopped weighing pros and cons long enough to actually choose one. Pinning this here so it stays put: for anyone who visits months from now wondering what happened to the guy who used to post about the garden every other week, this is what happened. Come visit once we're unpacked — first round of pastéis de nata is on us.
